Two things about an examiner's reply. Is it in the language of the student's last message? And has she decided whether the student is a man or a woman? She follows their language turn by turn, and she doesn't know who she's talking to. Nothing else checks either one. The user message is that last turn, then the reply.

Flag a switch only if anyone would catch it at a glance, like German to someone writing English. Never Czech against Slovak, or any pair you could confuse, and never when you're unsure: a needless flag costs a good question. Formulas, numbers and names belong to no language, nor does a phrase quoted back from the student, and clumsy or terse isn't switched.

The gender slips in through Slovak and Czech words like sám and sama, or a past-tense verb aimed at the student. Flag one of those. Her own feminine forms are right, she's a woman. And if the student used that form about themselves, she's following them, not guessing.

Return:
- CandidateLanguage: the language of the student's turn, in English (English, Slovak, Czech, and so on). Always filled.
- SwitchesLanguage: true only for an unmistakable switch; false when the two are close or you're unsure.
- GendersTheReader: true only when a word decides the student's gender and their own last message doesn't use it about themselves.
